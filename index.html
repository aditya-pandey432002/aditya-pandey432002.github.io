01

Fixing high-demand ticket booking on BookMyShow

NextLeap PM Fellowship capstone, 2024 · Ranked #1 of 200+

During Coldplay and Cricket World Cup sales, fans hit crashes, surprise logouts and long queues, then abandoned checkout, right as Zomato District came for the same events.

The lever
Conversion from event page to cart to payment: the one number a product change could move without adding server load.
What users said
21 surveyed. They wanted a live queue position; 62% were first-time concert-goers confused by seating tiers.
The call
A VIP tiered queue for loyal buyers, with a lottery that gives everyone else a fair second chance.
Full approach

Market context

Operating revenue grew from ₹277 Cr in FY22 to ₹1,400 Cr in FY24, with live events about a quarter of it. Growth was slowing, competition sharpened after Zomato bought Paytm Insider in August 2024, and customers switch platforms easily.

Research

  • Missing out on tickets was the top frustration; 71% of respondents were 18 to 34.
  • 62% were first-time concert-goers who didn't understand seat and price tiers.
  • Users wanted real-time queue position and availability to trust the process, and many wanted to book as a group.

Two personas anchored the design: Ankit, 25, an IT professional who books concerts and cricket often, and Riya, a student logged out mid-booking for Coldplay who lost her place.

How I chose

Four options scored on user value (30%), business impact (25%), feasibility (25%) and engagement (20%). The VIP tiered queue won: it rewards repeat buyers, keeps demand controllable, and the lottery protects fairness. Main risks: lottery fatigue and queue complexity.

Design

Wireframes cover the events home, an event page offering lottery entry or VIP access, a queue screen with live position and wait estimate, and checkout. The backend routes through an API gateway to event and booking services, then VIP eligibility logic into queue management and a separate lottery service.

Measurement

  • North star: ticket-sales conversion for VIP users as a share of available tickets.
  • Supporting: VIP activation rate, time to checkout, VIP retention across events, lottery re-entry after a loss.

Risks

  • Occasional buyers may feel it's unfair: reserve a fixed share of tickets for the lottery and give non-VIPs offers on future events.
  • Expensive tickets cause payment drop-off: add book-now, pay-later.

02

Neighbourhood car and bike sharing for Bengaluru

Product concept from primary research, 2025

People buy cars because public transport is unreliable, then pay EMIs and fuel on vehicles that sit idle most of the week. I tested whether neighbours would share them.

The insight
Interest wasn't the problem, trust was. People would share, but only inside their own community, with ID checks and insurance.
The call
A peer-to-peer platform for gated communities covering rentals and carpooling, two-wheelers included.
North star
Successful trips completed, backed by active listings, utilisation, match conversion and retention.
Full approach

The problem

Bengaluru has about 12.4 million registered vehicles, including roughly 2.5 million cars and 8.2 million two-wheelers. A 12 km commute can take hours, metro expansion lags demand, and the city is one of India's largest fuel consumers.

Who it's for

  • Stretched tech professionals (25 to 35) with high EMIs who want to earn from an idle car.
  • Students and temporary residents (18 to 25) who need flexible transport without buying.
  • Families in gated communities who often need a second car for a few hours.

Research

89% of non-owners were open to renting from a neighbour, 80% of owners were open to renting out an idle vehicle, 71% wanted insurance on every trip. Owners' top fears were damage and misuse. One resident said they'd never hand a car to a stranger from an app, but would to someone from their own complex verified by the residents' association.

MVP

  • Verification with Aadhaar or PAN, driving licence and community ID.
  • Listings for cars and two-wheelers with owner-set availability and pricing.
  • Search, filters, in-app payment; carpool matching for recurring commutes with live tracking.
  • In-app chat, mandatory ratings, basic insurance on every trip.

Go-to-market

Launch community by community: partner with residents' associations, run on-ground onboarding kiosks, give owners listing bonuses and renters first-trip discounts, and geo-target ads inside community boundaries.

03

Taking an organic skincare brand from $3.75M to $6M a month, without buying traffic

APM assignment for Virgio

Four months, limited acquisition spend, a $500K budget, and 2.5M monthly visitors already on the site. The growth had to come from the people already there.

The diagnosis
Only 2.5% of visitors bought, order value sat flat at $50 in a high-margin category, and 7 in 10 customers never came back.
The call
Nine initiatives across conversion, order value and retention, plus Refill Club: a refill subscription in reusable packaging.
The trade-off
$450K across seven initiatives, $50K held back. AI personalisation and influencer campaigns deferred.
Full approach

Three levers

  • Conversion, 2.5% to 4%: verified reviews with photos, shop-by-skin-type filters, a skin quiz that lands on a personalised routine.
  • Order value, $50 to $60: AM/PM and concern-based bundles, free shipping above $60, "complete your routine" cross-sells.
  • Retention, 30% to 50%: a tiered points programme, restock reminders at 30/60/90 days, post-purchase guides.

Refill Club

Buy a starter kit with a reusable bottle, then get refill pouches every 30, 60 or 90 days at 10 to 15% off with free shipping and loyalty points. Skincare is habitual, people forget to restock, and organic buyers care about packaging waste. The bottle adds a commitment that makes switching feel wasteful.

Rollout

  1. Month 1: pick three hero products, design the bottle, sign pouch suppliers.
  2. Month 2: launch the subscribe-and-save opt-in at checkout with email and WhatsApp reminders.
  3. Month 3: track refill conversion, A/B test reminder timing, scale if 25%+ keep reordering.

Every initiative went on an impact-vs-effort matrix. AI personalisation was deferred because simpler tools get most of the benefit in four months; influencer campaigns because they're expensive, unpredictable and do nothing for retention.
